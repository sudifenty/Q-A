## P4 TOPIC 1: PLANT LIFE

**1. What is a flowering plant?**
**Answer:** A flowering plant is a plant that produces flowers.

**2. Give two examples of flowering plants.**
**Answer:** Examples include maize, beans, hibiscus and mango.

**3. Why are flowers important to flowering plants?**
**Answer:** Flowers help flowering plants to reproduce by forming seeds, often after pollination and fertilisation.

**4. Name one flowering plant commonly grown in Uganda.**
**Answer:** Examples include maize, beans, hibiscus or mango.

**5. Name the main parts of a flower.**
**Answer:** The main parts are sepals, petals, stamens and the carpel (pistil).

**6. Which part of a flower protects it before it opens?**
**Answer:** The sepals protect the flower when it is still a bud.

**7. What is the function of the petals?**
**Answer:** Petals attract pollinators such as insects.

**8. Which flower part contains the ovules?**
**Answer:** The ovules are found in the ovary, which is part of the carpel.

**9. What are sepals?**
**Answer:** Sepals are the small, usually green parts that surround and protect a flower bud.

**10. What is the function of sepals?**
**Answer:** They protect the flower while it is still a bud.

**11. How do sepals help a young flower?**
**Answer:** They cover and protect the young flower from damage.

**12. What may happen if the sepals are damaged before a flower opens?**
**Answer:** The young flower may be damaged and may fail to develop normally.

**13. What are petals?**
**Answer:** Petals are the parts of a flower, often coloured, found inside the sepals.

**14. Give two functions of petals.**
**Answer:** They attract pollinators and help protect the reproductive parts of the flower.

**15. Why are many petals brightly coloured?**
**Answer:** Bright colours attract insects and other pollinators.

**16. How can petals help in pollination?**
**Answer:** They attract pollinators, which may carry pollen from one flower to another.

**17. What are stamens?**
**Answer:** Stamens are the male reproductive parts of a flower.

**18. Name the two main parts of a stamen.**
**Answer:** The anther and the filament.

**19. What is produced in the anther?**
**Answer:** Pollen grains are produced in the anther.

**20. Why are stamens important in reproduction?**
**Answer:** They produce pollen needed for reproduction.

**21. What is a carpel?**
**Answer:** A carpel is the female reproductive part of a flower.

**22. Name the main parts of a carpel.**
**Answer:** The stigma, style and ovary.

**23. What is the function of the stigma?**
**Answer:** The stigma receives pollen grains.

**24. What happens to the ovules after fertilisation?**
**Answer:** After fertilisation, the ovules develop into seeds.

**25. What is pollination?**
**Answer:** Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a flower.

**26. Name two agents of pollination.**
**Answer:** Examples are insects and wind.

**27. What is fertilisation in flowering plants?**
**Answer:** Fertilisation is the joining of the male cell from pollen with the female cell in the ovule.

**28. How is pollination different from fertilisation?**
**Answer:** Pollination is the transfer of pollen to the stigma; fertilisation is the joining of the male and female cells.

**29. What is a seed?**
**Answer:** A seed is a part formed from a fertilised ovule and can grow into a new plant.

**30. Where are seeds formed in a flowering plant?**
**Answer:** Seeds are formed inside the ovary, which usually develops into a fruit.

**31. Why are seeds important to plants?**
**Answer:** Seeds grow into new plants and help plants reproduce.

**32. Give four examples of seeds used as food.**
**Answer:** Beans, maize, peas and groundnuts.

**33. What is germination?**
**Answer:** Germination is the process by which a seed begins to grow into a seedling.

**34. Name three conditions needed for seed germination.**
**Answer:** Water, air (oxygen) and suitable warmth.

**35. Why does a seed need water during germination?**
**Answer:** Water softens the seed coat and helps the seed begin its growth processes.

**36. What happens to a seedling after germination?**
**Answer:** The seedling develops roots, a shoot and leaves as it grows.

**37. Mention five uses of plants to people.**
**Answer:** Plants provide food, medicine, timber, firewood and materials for making things.

**38. How do plants provide food?**
**Answer:** People eat plant parts such as roots, stems, leaves, fruits and seeds.

**39. How do plants provide materials for building or making things?**
**Answer:** Plants provide timber, poles, fibres and materials used to make paper, baskets and furniture.

**40. Why should useful plants be protected?**
**Answer:** Useful plants provide food and other materials, protect the environment and support people's livelihoods.

## P4 TOPIC 2: GROWING CROPS

**41. What is an annual crop?**
**Answer:** An annual crop is a crop that completes its life cycle within one year or one growing season.

**42. Give four examples of common annual crops.**
**Answer:** Maize, beans, rice and sorghum.

**43. Why are maize and beans called annual crops?**
**Answer:** They grow, produce seeds and complete their life cycle within one growing season.

**44. Name two annual crops commonly grown in Uganda.**
**Answer:** Maize and beans.

**45. Name the main requirements for growing maize.**
**Answer:** Suitable soil, water, sunlight, air, healthy seeds and proper care.

**46. What are two uses of maize?**
**Answer:** Maize is used as food for people and feed for animals.

**47. Why should maize be planted in fertile soil?**
**Answer:** Fertile soil supplies nutrients needed for healthy growth and good yields.

**48. Mention two problems that can reduce maize yield.**
**Answer:** Pests and diseases; drought or poor soil.

**49. What conditions are needed for growing beans?**
**Answer:** Suitable soil, water, warmth, sunlight and healthy seeds.

**50. Mention three uses of beans.**
**Answer:** Beans are eaten as food, sold for income and used to improve soil fertility when properly managed.

**51. Why should beans be protected from pests?**
**Answer:** Pests damage the plants and seeds and can reduce the harvest.

**52. How can a farmer improve bean production?**
**Answer:** Use healthy seeds, prepare the land well, plant at the right time, weed and control pests and diseases.

**53. What is sorghum?**
**Answer:** Sorghum is a cereal crop grown for its grain.

**54. Mention two uses of sorghum.**
**Answer:** It is used as food and animal feed; some varieties are used to make drinks.

**55. Why can sorghum be important in areas with less rainfall?**
**Answer:** It can tolerate drier conditions better than some other cereal crops.

**56. Name one product made from sorghum.**
**Answer:** Flour or porridge can be made from sorghum grain.

**57. Name five garden tools.**
**Answer:** Hoe, rake, panga, spade and watering can.

**58. What is a hoe used for?**
**Answer:** A hoe is used for digging, making planting holes and weeding.

**59. What is a rake used for?**
**Answer:** A rake is used for collecting rubbish and levelling soil.

**60. Why should garden tools be cleaned and stored safely?**
**Answer:** Cleaning and safe storage prevent rust, damage and accidents and help tools last longer.

**61. Mention the main steps followed when growing an annual crop.**
**Answer:** Clear the land, prepare the soil, select and plant seeds, water when needed, weed, control pests and diseases, and harvest at the right time.

**62. Why is land prepared before planting?**
**Answer:** Land is prepared to loosen the soil, remove weeds and make planting easier.

**63. Why is weeding important?**
**Answer:** Weeding reduces competition for water, light, space and nutrients.

**64. Why should crops be harvested at the right time?**
**Answer:** Harvesting at the right time helps prevent losses and gives good-quality produce.

**65. What is a crop disease?**
**Answer:** A crop disease is a condition that harms a crop and affects its normal growth.

**66. Mention two signs of disease in crops.**
**Answer:** Yellowing leaves, spots on leaves, wilting or rotting.

**67. How can crop diseases reduce food production?**
**Answer:** Diseases weaken or kill plants and reduce the amount and quality of produce.

**68. Give two ways of controlling crop diseases.**
**Answer:** Use healthy planting materials and resistant varieties; remove infected plants and seek appropriate control advice.

**69. What is a crop pest?**
**Answer:** A crop pest is an organism that damages crops.

**70. Give four examples of crop pests.**
**Answer:** Locusts, aphids, caterpillars and weevils.

**71. How do pests damage crops?**
**Answer:** They may eat leaves, stems, roots, flowers, fruits or seeds.

**72. Mention two ways of controlling crop pests.**
**Answer:** Remove pests by hand where safe and suitable; use traps or recommended pest-control methods.

**73. Why should pests and diseases be controlled?**
**Answer:** To protect crops and improve the quantity and quality of the harvest.

**74. Mention safe methods of controlling crop pests.**
**Answer:** Use hand-picking, traps, crop rotation, field sanitation and only approved chemicals according to instructions.

**75. Why should farmers avoid careless use of chemicals?**
**Answer:** Chemicals can poison people and animals, contaminate food and water, and harm useful organisms.

**76. How can good field hygiene reduce pests and diseases?**
**Answer:** Removing weeds and crop remains can reduce places where pests and disease-causing organisms survive.

**77. Why should crops be watered when necessary?**
**Answer:** Water helps seeds germinate and plants grow, especially during dry periods.

**78. Why is thinning sometimes done?**
**Answer:** Thinning reduces overcrowding so that remaining plants get enough space, light, water and nutrients.

**79. Why should farmers remove weeds?**
**Answer:** Weeds compete with crops for water, light, space and nutrients.

**80. How does proper crop care increase yield?**
**Answer:** Healthy crops receive the conditions and care they need, so they are more likely to produce a good harvest.

## P4 TOPIC 3: WEATHER CHANGES AROUND US

**81. What is weather?**
**Answer:** Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a place over a short period.

**82. Mention four elements of weather.**
**Answer:** Temperature, rainfall, wind and cloud cover.

**83. How can weather affect people's daily activities?**
**Answer:** Weather affects farming, clothing, travel, drying clothes and outdoor activities.

**84. Why is weather observation useful?**
**Answer:** It helps people plan activities and farmers plan farm work.

**85. What is a change in weather?**
**Answer:** A change in weather is a difference in conditions such as temperature, wind, cloud cover or rainfall.

**86. Mention signs that weather is changing.**
**Answer:** Clouds gathering, wind increasing, temperature changing or rain beginning.

**87. Why can the weather change during the day?**
**Answer:** Sunlight, wind, cloud cover and changes in the atmosphere can cause weather conditions to change.

**88. How can sudden weather changes affect farmers?**
**Answer:** It may delay planting or harvesting, damage crops or cause flooding and soil erosion.

**89. What is the water cycle?**
**Answer:** The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between the land, water bodies and the atmosphere.

**90. Name the main stages of the water cycle.**
**Answer:** Evaporation, condensation and precipitation (rainfall).

**91. What causes water to evaporate?**
**Answer:** Heat from the Sun causes liquid water to change into water vapour.

**92. How does rainfall return water to the land?**
**Answer:** Water droplets in clouds join and fall to the ground as rain.

**93. What is rainfall?**
**Answer:** Rainfall is water falling from clouds to the ground in drops.

**94. Name two forms in which water may fall from clouds.**
**Answer:** Rain and hail; in some places, snow also falls.

**95. How is rainfall measured?**
**Answer:** Rainfall is measured using a rain gauge, usually in millimetres.

**96. Why is rainfall important to crops?**
**Answer:** Rain supplies water needed for crop growth.

**97. What is a rain gauge?**
**Answer:** A rain gauge is an instrument used to collect and measure rainfall.

**98. What does a rain gauge measure?**
**Answer:** It measures the amount of rainfall.

**99. Where should a rain gauge be placed for useful readings?**
**Answer:** Place it in an open area away from trees and buildings, where it can collect rain without obstruction.

**100. Why should the reading of a rain gauge be recorded regularly?**
**Answer:** Regular records help show how much rain has fallen and how rainfall changes over time.

**101. What is a weather chart?**
**Answer:** A weather chart is a table or chart used to record weather observations.

**102. What information can be shown on a simple weather chart?**
**Answer:** It may show rainfall, temperature, wind, cloud cover and the daily weather condition.

**103. Why do learners record weather observations?**
**Answer:** Records help learners compare weather conditions and identify changes over time.

**104. How can a weather chart help a farmer?**
**Answer:** It helps a farmer plan planting, watering, spraying and harvesting.

**105. Why is sunshine important?**
**Answer:** Sunshine provides light and warmth needed by plants and helps people dry clothes and crops.

**106. How can sunshine affect drying?**
**Answer:** Sunshine provides heat that helps water evaporate and wet materials dry.

**107. How can too much sunshine affect crops?**
**Answer:** Too much sunshine can dry the soil and cause plants to wilt.

**108. What instrument or method can be used to observe sunshine in simple weather work?**
**Answer:** Observe and record the hours or periods of sunshine; a sunshine recorder may be used where available.

**109. What is wind?**
**Answer:** Wind is moving air.

**110. How can wind be observed?**
**Answer:** Observe the movement of leaves, flags or smoke, or use a wind vane to observe direction.

**111. Mention two effects of strong winds.**
**Answer:** Strong winds can damage buildings and crops and blow down trees.

**112. Why can wind be useful to people?**
**Answer:** Wind can help dry clothes and crops, move sailing boats and turn windmills or turbines.

**113. What are clouds?**
**Answer:** Clouds are collections of tiny water droplets or ice crystals in the atmosphere.

**114. How can clouds give clues about coming rain?**
**Answer:** Dark, thick clouds may indicate that rain is likely.

**115. Why do clouds form?**
**Answer:** Clouds form when moist air cools and water vapour condenses into tiny droplets or ice crystals.

**116. Why are clouds important in the water cycle?**
**Answer:** Clouds hold condensed water that may later fall as precipitation.

**117. How can people keep safe during heavy rain or strong winds?**
**Answer:** Stay indoors or in a safe building, avoid flooded places and do not shelter under isolated trees during storms.

**118. What should a farmer consider before spraying crops?**
**Answer:** The farmer should consider wind direction and speed, expected rain and the safety instructions for the product.

**119. Why should children avoid playing in flooded areas?**
**Answer:** Floodwater may be deep, fast-moving or contaminated and can cause drowning or disease.

**120. How can weather information help people plan activities?**
**Answer:** Weather information helps people choose suitable times for farming, travelling, drying produce and other activities.

## P4 TOPIC 4: PERSONAL HYGIENE

**121. What is personal hygiene?**

**Answer:** Personal hygiene is the practice of keeping the body and personal belongings clean to maintain health.

**122. Mention five personal hygiene practices.**

**Answer:** Examples include bathing, brushing teeth, washing hands, trimming nails and wearing clean clothes.

**123. Why is personal hygiene important?**

**Answer:** It helps prevent diseases, removes dirt and body odour, and keeps a person healthy.

**124. How can poor hygiene affect a learner?**

**Answer:** A learner may get infections, feel uncomfortable, smell bad or be teased by others.

**125. How should a person keep the body clean?**

**Answer:** Bathe regularly with clean water and soap, and wash the body properly.

**126. Why should the body be washed regularly?**

**Answer:** Washing removes sweat, dirt and germs from the skin.

**127. Why should the hair be kept clean?**

**Answer:** Clean hair helps prevent dirt, bad smell and some scalp problems.

**128. What can happen when the body is not kept clean?**

**Answer:** The person may develop body odour, skin problems or infections.

**129. Why should we bathe regularly?**

**Answer:** Regular bathing removes sweat, dirt and germs and helps keep the skin healthy.

**130. What can be used for bathing?**

**Answer:** Clean water, soap, a washcloth or sponge, and a clean towel may be used.

**131. Why should a clean towel be used?**

**Answer:** A clean towel helps avoid transferring dirt and germs back to the body.

**132. How does bathing help prevent body odour?**

**Answer:** Bathing washes away sweat and bacteria that can cause body odour.

**133. Why should teeth be cleaned every day?**

**Answer:** Cleaning teeth removes food particles and plaque and helps prevent tooth decay and bad breath.

**134. What should be used to clean teeth?**

**Answer:** A toothbrush and toothpaste, or another safe recommended tooth-cleaning method, should be used.

**135. How can dirty teeth cause problems?**

**Answer:** Dirty teeth can develop tooth decay, gum disease and bad breath.

**136. Why should sugary foods be taken in moderation?**

**Answer:** Too much sugar encourages tooth decay and may contribute to poor health.

**137. When should hands be washed?**

**Answer:** Wash hands before preparing or eating food, after using the toilet, after handling waste, and after touching dirty things or animals.

**138. Why should hands be washed before eating?**

**Answer:** Handwashing removes germs that could enter the mouth with food.

**139. Why should hands be washed after using a toilet?**

**Answer:** It removes germs from the hands and helps prevent the spread of intestinal diseases.

**140. How does handwashing help prevent disease?**

**Answer:** It removes germs from the hands before they can enter the body or spread to other people and surfaces.

**141. Why should clothes be kept clean?**

**Answer:** Clean clothes remove dirt and sweat, help prevent skin problems and make a person comfortable.

**142. How should dirty clothes be handled?**

**Answer:** Keep dirty clothes separate from clean clothes and wash them with soap and clean water.

**143. Why should clothes be dried properly after washing?**

**Answer:** Proper drying helps prevent dampness, unpleasant smells and the growth of some fungi.

**144. What problems may result from wearing dirty clothes?**

**Answer:** They may cause body odour, skin irritation or infections and attract pests.

**145. Why should bedding be washed and aired?**

**Answer:** Washing and airing bedding removes dirt, sweat and some germs and helps keep it fresh.

**146. Mention examples of bedding.**

**Answer:** Examples are bedsheets, blankets, pillowcases, pillows and mattresses.

**147. How can dirty bedding affect health?**

**Answer:** Dirty bedding may contribute to skin irritation, unpleasant smells and the spread of some infections or pests.

**148. Why is sunlight useful when airing bedding?**

**Answer:** Sunlight helps dry bedding and may reduce some germs and dampness.

**149. Why should people avoid sharing personal items such as toothbrushes?**

**Answer:** Sharing them can transfer germs, lice or other infections from one person to another.

**150. Name two personal items that should not be shared.**

**Answer:** Examples are toothbrushes and towels. Combs and razors should also not be shared.

**151. How should personal items be stored?**

**Answer:** Keep personal items clean, dry and in a clean, separate place.

**152. Why should combs and towels be kept clean?**

**Answer:** They can collect dirt and germs and transfer them to the body or hair.

**153. How can poor hygiene spread disease?**

**Answer:** Germs can pass through dirty hands, contaminated food or water, dirty clothing and unclean surroundings.

**154. Mention two diseases associated with poor hygiene.**

**Answer:** Examples include diarrhoea and some skin infections. Poor hygiene can also help spread intestinal worms.

**155. How can good hygiene reduce disease?**

**Answer:** Good hygiene removes or reduces germs and lowers the chance of infection.

**156. Why is cleanliness important at school?**

**Answer:** It helps learners stay healthy, prevents the spread of disease and creates a pleasant place for learning.

**157. Describe a simple daily hygiene routine for a learner.**

**Answer:** A learner can bathe, brush the teeth, wash hands with soap, wear clean clothes, trim nails and keep personal items clean.

**158. Why should nails be trimmed?**

**Answer:** Short nails collect less dirt and are easier to clean, reducing the spread of germs.

**159. Why should shoes and socks be kept clean?**

**Answer:** Clean shoes and socks help reduce bad smells and may prevent some foot problems.

**160. How can a learner encourage good hygiene at home?**

**Answer:** A learner can practise good hygiene, explain its benefits, remind family members kindly and help keep shared areas clean.
